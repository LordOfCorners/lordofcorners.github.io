A Journey Through Poland · September 2026

In Adam's Footsteps

Fifteen days across Poland

This itinerary covers fifteen days across Poland — Warsaw, Gdańsk, Łódź, Kraków, and the Tatra mountains. It's a trip built around great cities, good food, and personal exploration. Running through it is a family thread: My Journey: Memoirs of a Lucky Man, the memoir of Adam Kahane, who was born in Poland in 1922, survived deportation to Soviet labor camps, and emigrated to New York in 1948. Some of the places on this trip are places he lived. Others are just places worth going.

I. Warsaw Sep 4–7 · 3 Nights · Polonia Palace Hotel
Warsaw was 85% destroyed by the Nazis — deliberately, building by building — and rebuilt from photographs and paintings after the war. When Adam returned to Poland from the Soviet Union in January 1947, his train stopped a mile short of the ruined station. The city you walk through today is an act of collective memory.
Fri Sep 4
Arrive Warsaw · First Dinner in Poland

Arrive 1:00pm from JFK. Check in to Polonia Palace Hotel — a historic 4-star hotel that opened in 1913, Warsaw's second oldest, once frequented by diplomats and artists and where General Eisenhower stayed in September 1945. Walk the Old Town, rebuilt brick by brick from photographs after the Nazis destroyed 85% of the city. First dinner in Poland.

Sat Sep 5
POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews

One of the finest museums in Europe — a 1,000-year story told with extraordinary care. Plan at least 3 hours. Essential context for everything that follows: the Jewish world that existed in Poland before the war, its annihilation, and the handful of survivors.

From the memoir

"Of all the children shown in this picture, I am the only one left. Most did not survive the war."

— Adam Kahane, captioning a family photograph from Jasło
Sun Sep 6
Warsaw Uprising Museum · Praga

The Warsaw Uprising Museum tells the story of the 1944 uprising against Nazi occupation — what was happening in Poland while Adam and his family were surviving in Soviet labor camps and collective farms in Kazakhstan. Afternoon: cross the Vistula to Praga, Warsaw's bohemian neighbourhood — one of the few districts the Nazis didn't level, so the tenements are genuinely prewar. The Neon Museum and the Koneser distillery complex are both there.

Mon Sep 7
Morning Free · Train to Gdańsk

Morning free. Train to Gdańsk departs 12:29pm from Warszawa Centralna, arrives Gdańsk Główny 3:01pm (2h 32m) — EIP 3506, car 1, 1st class, seat 62 (window). Booked — arrives right around hotel check-in

II. Gdańsk Sep 7–10 · 3 Nights · Gotyk House
Poland's great Baltic port city — a beautifully preserved Hanseatic old town, the birthplace of the Solidarity movement, and the city where WWII began when Germany invaded on September 1, 1939.
Mon Sep 7
Arrive Gdańsk · Old Town · Waterfront

Arrive 3:01pm. Check in to Gotyk House — located in the oldest tenement building in Gdańsk, dating to 1451, in the heart of the Old Town. Walk the Long Market (Długi Targ), the colourful Hanseatic waterfront, the medieval crane on the Motława river. Seafood dinner — Gdańsk is a Baltic port city and the fish is excellent.

Tue Sep 8
Malbork Castle Day Trip

One of the most impressive medieval castles in the world — a vast 13th-century Teutonic fortress about an hour by train from Gdańsk. Plan a full day.

Wed Sep 9
Possibly Sopot · European Solidarity Centre · Seafood Dinner

Optional morning trip to Sopot — 20 minutes by commuter train, a charming Belle Époque resort town with the longest wooden pier in Europe. Afternoon: the European Solidarity Centre, a superb museum telling the story of the Solidarity movement that brought down communism in Poland. Evening: one more seafood dinner on the Motława.

Thu Sep 10
Morning Free · Train to Łódź

Morning free in Gdańsk. Train departs 11:47am, arrives Łódź 4:29pm (4h 42m) — IC 5114, straight through to Łódź Fabryczna with no change, car 11, 1st class, seat 66. Booked — no connection to make

III. Łódź Sep 10–12 · 2 Nights · PURO Łódź
Adam's connection: He spent the first five years of his life here before his mother moved them to Jasło. His father Jakub ran a profitable pharmacy and pharmaceutical lab in Łódź. Adam returned here January–August 1947, running his father's pharmacy after the war. Jakub died on the train to Auschwitz from Łódź at age 54.
Thu Sep 10
Arrive Łódź · Piotrkowska Street · Dinner

Arrive 4:29pm at Łódź Fabryczna — a breathtaking modern station. Check in to PURO Łódź, steps from Manufaktura. Walk Piotrkowska Street, lined with Art Nouveau buildings stretching 4km through the city. Dinner and drinks.

From the memoir

"As I entered the pharmacy, I saw Uncle Iziu. He kissed me and said, 'This place is yours. I will help you run it for a while.'"

— Adam Kahane, on returning to Łódź in January 1947
Fri Sep 11
Jewish Cemetery · Off Piotrkowska · Manufaktura

The Łódź Jewish Cemetery is one of the largest in the world — over 180,000 graves, including the world's largest Jewish mausoleum. Adam's father Jakub operated his pharmacy in the Łódź ghetto almost until its final liquidation. In the afternoon, Off Piotrkowska — a once-abandoned factory now buzzing with cafés, bars and studios, identified by CNN as "Europe's hippest micro-scene." Evening at Manufaktura.

From the memoir

"My father stayed in Łódź during the occupation. Luckily his pharmacy was in the area designated as ghetto and he operated it almost to the time of the final liquidation of the ghetto. I was told by an Auschwitz survivor that he died of a heart attack on the train to the death camp. He was only 54."

— Adam Kahane
Sat Sep 12
Morning Wander · Train to Kraków

Morning wander — murals, woonerfs, the Museum of the City of Łódź in Poznański's Neo-Baroque palace. Board at Łódź Widzew, not Fabryczna — it's a taxi ride out from the center, unlike the station you arrived at. Train departs 2:04pm, arrives Kraków 4:30pm (2h 26m) — IC 8322.

IV. Kraków Sep 12–16 · 4 Nights · The Warszauer
Adam's connection: He lived at Świętego Sebastiana 5 — steps from Kazimierz, the historic Jewish quarter — in 1947, the year he left Poland for Paris. The Wistreich family (Reggie, Eva, Hugo, Aunt Hanka, Uncle Staszek) also lived in Kraków after returning from Kazakhstan. He visited again in 1976.
Sat Sep 12
Arrive Kraków · First Evening in Kazimierz

Arrive 4:30pm. Check in to The Warszauer — a 10-room boutique hotel in Kazimierz with a rooftop terrace overlooking the historic Jewish quarter. Walk to Świętego Sebastiana 5 — Adam's address in 1947, a short walk from the hotel. Spend the evening in Kazimierz: synagogues, Jewish bookshops, restaurants, bars.

Dinner at Starka at 6:00pm — a traditional Polish restaurant in Kazimierz, known for pierogi, beef cheeks, and house-infused vodkas. Booked — 6:00pm, table for 3

From the memoir

"We concluded that there is no future for us in communist Poland and decided to emigrate. The pharmacy was very profitable but I got only $2000 when I sold it... We left Poland in September 1947 after a year's stay."

— Adam Kahane, on leaving Kraków for Paris
Sun Sep 13
Schindler's Factory

Schindler's Enamel Factory at 10am — guided tour booked for three. A superb WWII museum telling the story of Kraków's Jews under Nazi occupation. The tour runs roughly 90 minutes; arrive 15 minutes early at ul. Lipowa 4. Booked — 10am guided tour, 3 tickets

Done by about 11:45, and the afternoon is open. Everything below is optional — pick what the day has energy for. The first three are all in Podgórze, within a few minutes' walk of the factory.

  • Eagle Pharmacy — Plac Bohaterów Getta 18, about 7 minutes' walk. The only pharmacy inside the Kraków ghetto; Tadeusz Pankiewicz, a Catholic Pole, refused to leave when the walls went up around it and supplied medicine, hiding places and information until the liquidation. 45 minutes to an hour and a quarter, ~18–20 zł. Open Wed–Sun only, so this Sunday is the one realistic window on the whole trip. The obvious counterpart to Jakub's pharmacy in Łódź — one man chose to be inside, the other had the ghetto built around him.
  • Plac Bohaterów Getta — the square itself, between the two museums. The ghetto's assembly point, where deportations were staged. Now a field of empty steel chairs. You pass through it either way; worth stopping in.
  • Galicia Jewish Museum — about 600m on. Photography-led, and concerned with what survives of Jewish Galicia today rather than with the destruction. Jakub was born in Grzymałów, in Galicia, which gives this one a direct line.
  • Wawel Royal Castle — a change of register entirely, and about 25 minutes back across the river. The historic seat of Polish kings above the Vistula. The interiors are ticketed separately by route: State Rooms, Royal Private Apartments, Crown Treasury, Armoury, Wawel Underground and Lost Wawel, with a combined Wawel for Enthusiasts pass covering the lot. The Cathedral is a separate ticket run by the church, not the museum. Book ahead — slots release about a month out and the State Rooms go first.

Lunch: Krakus, a proper old-school bar mleczny at ul. Limanowskiego 16, a few minutes from the factory and right by Plac Bohaterów Getta. Canteen-style — you order at the counter and they shout your name when it's ready — packed with locals, almost no tourists, and startlingly cheap. English menu if you ask. Open 9am–4pm on Sundays.

Mon Sep 14
Wieliczka Salt Mine

Wieliczka Salt Mine — a UNESCO site where seven centuries of miners carved chapels, underground lakes and an entire cathedral out of rock salt. St. Kinga's Chapel, cut whole from the salt, is the one everyone comes for. Tourist Route, English, 11:30am, about two and a half hours. Meeting point is Daniłowicza 10, roughly 30 minutes from Kraków by taxi or train from Kraków Główny. A deliberately late start after Schindler's the day before, with the afternoon left free. Booked — 11:30am English tour, 3 tickets

Three things worth knowing: it's a constant 14–17°C underground, so everyone brings a jacket whatever the weather above. The descent is around 800 steps — the way out is by lift. And the two discount tickets need proof of age at entry, so bring passports. Closed comfortable shoes; large bags aren't allowed in.

Tue Sep 15
Flex Day

The last full day in Kraków, deliberately left open — whatever hasn't happened yet lands here. Candidates, in rough order of likelihood:

Kazimierz on foot — the quarter you've been sleeping in all week, walked properly: the seven historic synagogues, Szeroka Street, the ghetto wall fragments across the river in Podgórze, Plac Bohaterów Getta. Works either way — book a guided Jewish heritage walk, or just do it yourselves at your own pace with the map and stop when you feel like it. Half a day, no travel, and it connects directly to Adam's year in this city. Currently the most likely option.

Jasło — about 2 hours by train. Adam's hometown, where he grew up from age 5 to 17. The house he lived in is gone — Jasło was almost entirely destroyed in the war — but something was rebuilt on the spot, and Adam went back and stood in front of it in the seventies. The Jasiołka river where he and Reggie kayaked and baked potatoes in the ashes. Monastery Hill where he had his first kiss. The Boys' High School from which he was expelled just before the war began.

The Jasło Rynek in a prewar postcard.
Adam and Gert at the rebuilt family house on the Jasło Rynek, circa 1976. The ground floor had been converted to a state butcher's shop under communist Poland.
From the memoir

"On the other bank of the river were a meadow and a small grove. It was so pretty and peaceful there. Many years later, when I wanted to think about something relaxing, I thought about this place."

— Adam Kahane
Adam and friends on the Jasiołka river.

The family that filled this town with life was almost entirely lost in the war. Uncle Dulek, Aunt Manka, cousins Renatka and Jasia — Adam's closest companions growing up — were among those who didn't survive. Of the children in the family photograph from Jasło, he was the only one left.

Auschwitz-Birkenau — currently the least likely of the four, but it belongs on the list. Adam's Uncle Mundek and his wife were taken here after fleeing Vienna. Adam's father Jakub almost certainly died on the train to this camp at age 54. If it happens it needs a guided tour booked well in advance, so decide early rather than on the day. Book guided tour weeks in advance at auschwitz.org

Wawel Royal Castle — if it didn't happen on Sunday. The seat of Polish kings, high above the Vistula, walkable from Kazimierz and the one thing on this list that isn't about the war. Each route is its own ticket — State Rooms, Royal Private Apartments, Crown Treasury, Armoury, Wawel Underground, Lost Wawel — or take the combined Wawel for Enthusiasts pass. The Cathedral is booked separately through the church. Slots release about a month out and the State Rooms sell out first, so whichever day this lands on, book it in advance rather than turning up. Book ahead — bilety.wawel.krakow.pl

From the memoir — Uncle Mundek

"After his retirement they lived in Vienna. When Austria was annexed they moved to Holland — he was a Dutch citizen — and from there they were eventually taken to Auschwitz."

— Adam Kahane

V. Zakopane Sep 16–18 · 2 Nights · Aries Hotel & SPA
Poland's mountain capital, gateway to the Tatras. Adam skied here as a boy and went on to run a successful ski shop in Washington D.C. for over 50 years — but you're here for the mountains, which are reason enough.
Wed Sep 16
Arrive Zakopane · Krupówki · Gubałówka

Arrive about 12:30pm. Check-in at the Aries isn't until 3:00pm — leave the bags at reception and go straight out. Krupówki is a five-minute walk: lunch there, then wander Zakopane's pedestrian street, lined with stalls selling oscypek (smoked sheep's cheese), mountain food and crafts. The Tatra peaks rise dramatically on every side.

Gubałówka in the afternoon — the funicular runs up from the top of Krupówki to a ridge with a panoramic view straight across at the Tatras, plus stalls, grilled food and a toboggan run at the top. No booking needed, just walk up and buy tickets. An easy first afternoon that gets you the big mountain view without a hike.

Back to the Aries after 3:00pm to check in properly — pool, outdoor jacuzzis, sauna, breakfast included. Dinner in town.

From the memoir

"In 1935 we took part in a sightseeing camp in the mountains. About 40 boys from all over Poland took part in the camp. We were the only Jewish boys... We hiked in high mountains and took a boat trip through a picturesque gorge. I will never forget how good the food tasted."

— Adam Kahane, on his mountain camp as a teenager
Adam and friends skiing near Jasło, 1930s.
Thu Sep 17
Morskie Oko Hike

The crown jewel of the Tatras — a glacial lake at 1,395 metres, surrounded by soaring peaks. The e-bus runs all the way to Włosienica, past the usual Palenica Białczańska trailhead, which cuts the walk down to about 1.5km each way instead of the full two-hour approach. That is what makes this a comfortable day for three rather than a long slog.

Two fixed points, and the rest is yours: the 9:00am e-bus out from the TPN Nature Education Center (ul. Chałubińskiego 42), and the 3:00pm bus back from Włosienica. Arrange the ride to the pickup with the front desk the night before — Uber and Bolt are unreliable in Zakopane that early, and a local bus works too. The ride out is about 40 minutes, then a 20–30 minute walk to the lake, which leaves roughly four hours there.

Things worth knowing before you go:

  • The PTTK mountain hut restaurant at the lake is cash only — bring złoty. Open 9am–6pm in low season.
  • If Michael wants the side trip up to Czarny Staw pod Rysami, it's 1.5–2 hours round trip on a steep but moderate marked trail. This is not the Rysy summit, which is a serious technical climb — do not confuse the two.
  • Keep the ticket PDF on all three phones. Signal in the park is unreliable.

Back at the Aries afterwards for the pool, jacuzzis and sauna.

Booked — E-bus 3 out / 3 back · 89 zł & 78 zł each · TPN entry included · PDF on phones
Fri Sep 18
Kasprowy Wierch Cable Car · Train to Kraków

Check out before leaving and arrange luggage storage with reception — you won't be back for the bags until around midday. Kuźnice is a short taxi or bus ride from the center; sort out which the night before.

Cable car up Kasprowy Wierch, 9:30am slot — be at the base station by 9:10am. ID is checked 20 minutes before the slot and there's a boarding queue after that, so this is the one hard deadline of the day. Michael is the named passenger on all three tickets and the one whose ID gets verified — bring his passport. Booked — 9:30am cable car

You get a fixed 1 hour 40 minutes on top. Lunch is at Poziom 1959, the summit restaurant — eat up there rather than in town. Wander the summit area and step out along the ridge a little if everyone's comfortable, but the full traverse toward Kopa Kondracka and Giewont is a serious multi-hour hike and isn't the plan.

Back down late morning, collect the bags, and on to the station. Train departs 2:00pm, arrives Kraków 3:57pm (1h 57m) — EIC 3550, 1st class. There's comfortable margin between the cable car and the train. Booked — WH63519693

A Note on the Memoir

My Journey: Memoirs of a Lucky Man by Adam Kahane describes his childhood on the Jasło Rynek, his summers on the Jasiołka river, his deportation to Soviet labor camps, his postwar life in Łódź and Kraków, and his emigration to New York via Paris in 1948. He wrote it at the age of 90. Of the children in the family photograph from Jasło that appears in its pages, he was the only one who survived the war.

Some of the places on this trip are places he knew well. Some things are gone. Some, remarkably, are still there — including the house on the Rynek with the light red roof, the river on whose banks he spent his summers, and the mountains where he skied as a boy.

"Be optimistic. Eventually everything works out. Optimists live longer and enjoy life more. Hope you all will have as lucky and happy a life as I did."

— Adam Kahane, final words of his memoir, written at age 92
